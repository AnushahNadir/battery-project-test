NASA PCoE Battery Dataset Reference

DATASET OVERVIEW
The NASA Prognostics Center of Excellence (PCoE) Battery Dataset (Saha & Goebel, 2007) is one
of the most widely used public benchmarks for battery RUL prediction research. It contains
complete charge-discharge-impedance cycle data for Li-ion 18650 cells tested to end-of-life
under controlled laboratory conditions. Dataset source: ti.arc.nasa.gov/tech/dash/groups/pcoe/
prognostic-data-repository/. This dataset is distinct from the CALCE dataset (Center for
Advanced Life Cycle Engineering, University of Maryland), which uses different cell chemistries,
capacities, and testing protocols.

EXPERIMENTAL SETUP
Cell chemistry: Lithium cobalt oxide (LiCoO2) cathode, graphite anode.
Nominal capacity: 2.0 Ah (rated), though measured initial capacities vary by cell
(range across 34 cells: approximately 1.02-1.92 Ah at first discharge cycle).
Form factor: 18650 cylindrical cells.
Operating temperatures: Room (~24 degrees C), elevated (43 degrees C), low (4 degrees C).
End-of-life criterion: Capacity falls below 80% of each cell's own measured first-cycle
capacity (alpha=0.80). This is a per-cell threshold, not a fixed Ah cutoff. Across 34 cells
this corresponds to approximately 0.81-1.53 Ah (mean 1.23 Ah).

BATTERY GROUPS AND TEMPERATURE COHORTS
34 cells total (B0005-B0056, non-contiguous):
- Room temperature (~24 degrees C): B0005, B0006, B0007, B0018, B0025-B0028, B0033-B0036
- Hot (43 degrees C): B0029-B0032 (censored, did not reach EOL before experiment end)
- Cold (4 degrees C): B0049-B0056 (accelerated degradation)

CHARGING PROTOCOL
Constant current (CC) charging at 1.5A until terminal voltage reaches 4.2V, then constant
voltage (CV) charging until current drops to 20mA.

DISCHARGE PROTOCOLS
- Batteries 5, 6, 7, 18: 2A discharge to 2.7V cutoff
- Batteries 25-28: 2A discharge to varying cutoffs
- Batteries 33, 34: 4A discharge (B33 to 2.0V, B34 to 2.2V)
- Battery 36: 2A discharge to 2.7V
- Batteries 38-40: accelerated aging protocols
- Batteries 41-56: various stress protocols including temperature variation
Higher discharge current rates (4A vs 2A) accelerate degradation.

DATA STRUCTURE
Each battery's data is organized as cycles. Measurement channels: Voltage_measured,
Current_measured, Temperature_measured, Current_charge, Voltage_charge, Time (within cycle).
Impedance cycles include frequency sweep data (0.1 Hz to 5 kHz).

CYCLE COUNTS AND LIFETIMES
- Standard 2A discharge (room temp): 150-300 cycles to end-of-life
- Accelerated 4A discharge: 100-200 cycles
- Cold temperature: accelerated degradation, shorter lifetimes
- Hot temperature (censored cells): did not reach EOL within observation window

EARLY-CYCLE BEHAVIOR
Some batteries show anomalous early-cycle capacity variance due to formation cycling artifacts
or temperature transients. Residual spike anomalies in early cycles are not necessarily
indicative of accelerated failure.

KNOWN ANOMALIES
- B0055: Unusual impedance growth, overconfident calibration. LOBO calibration recommended.
- Cold-group batteries (B0049-B0056): faster degradation, highest cross-battery RMSE,
  most susceptible to conformal coverage violations under distribution shift.
- Missing cycles in some batteries due to test equipment interruptions.

CAPACITY MEASUREMENT
Discharge capacity = integral(I * dt). This is the most reliable measurement.
All capacity values are measured discharge capacity in Ah.

CROSS-BATTERY PREDICTION CHALLENGE
Cross-battery RUL prediction trains on a subset of cells and tests on entirely unseen cells.
This is harder than within-battery prediction. Within-battery methods observe the target
cell's early degradation trajectory; cross-battery methods must generalize from population
patterns without seeing any cycles from the test cell. Distribution shift between training
and test batteries is the primary source of conformal coverage violations.

USE IN RESEARCH
Reported errors depend heavily on task definition. Within-battery methods: 1-10 cycle RMSE.
Cross-battery methods: 20-65 cycle RMSE depending on thermal group. Cold-group cells far
from training distribution produce the highest errors and lowest conformal coverage.
